Mission Timeline

From Test Flight to Moon Base

The Artemis Program unfolds in deliberate steps — each mission building on the last, from uncrewed hardware tests to sustained human presence on another world.

November 2022
Artemis I Complete

The first integrated test of SLS and Orion. This uncrewed mission proved both systems could perform as designed in the real lunar environment before risking a crew.

  • SLS launched from Kennedy Space Center
  • Orion entered lunar orbit for approximately 2 weeks
  • Traveled 40,000 miles beyond the Moon — farther than any spacecraft built for humans
  • Splashdown: December 11, 2022 after 25.5 days
  • All mission objectives achieved
👨‍🚀
2026 (Target)
Artemis II In Progress

The first crewed Artemis flight. Four astronauts fly around the Moon without landing — the first humans at lunar distance since Apollo 17 in December 1972.

  • Crew: Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, Jeremy Hansen
  • First woman and first person of color to travel to the Moon
  • First Canadian astronaut in deep space
  • 10-day total mission duration
  • Free-return trajectory around the Moon — no landing
🌕
2027 (Target)
Artemis III Planned

The first crewed lunar landing since Apollo 17. Two astronauts descend to the south pole aboard SpaceX Starship HLS while two remain in Orion at the Gateway.

  • First Moon landing in over 50 years
  • First landing at the lunar south pole
  • Target site: near Shackleton Crater
  • Up to 4 lunar EVAs (moonwalks) planned
  • First woman to walk on the Moon
🛰️
2028 (Target)
Artemis IV Planned

First mission to involve the Lunar Gateway. SLS Block 1B delivers the international habitation module (I-HAB) developed by ESA and JAXA.

  • First docking with Lunar Gateway
  • Delivers ESA and JAXA I-HAB module
  • First crewed flight of SLS Block 1B
  • Extended surface operations at the south pole
🔭
2029 and Beyond
Artemis V+ Planned

From Artemis V onward the program transitions from exploration to sustained operations — building the infrastructure needed for long-duration stays and eventually Mars.

  • Increasing surface stay durations
  • Lunar terrain vehicle operations
  • In-situ resource utilization (water ice to fuel)
  • Gateway becomes a permanent international outpost
  • Technology validation for the journey to Mars
Historical Context: The last time humans walked on the Moon was December 14, 1972, when Apollo 17 astronauts Gene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt departed Taurus–Littrow. For over 50 years the Moon has been visited only by robotic probes. Artemis ends the longest gap between human lunar visits in history.
