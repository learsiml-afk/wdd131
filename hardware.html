The Machines

Artemis Hardware

Four revolutionary systems work in concert to carry astronauts from Earth's surface to the Moon and back. Here is how they all fit together.

Mission Architecture: SLS launches Orion → Orion transits to lunar orbit → docks with Lunar Gateway → crew transfers to HLS → HLS descends to surface → crew explores → HLS returns to Gateway → Orion returns to Earth.
Launch Vehicle

Space Launch System (SLS)

The most powerful operational rocket in history, succeeding the Saturn V. SLS uses four RS-25 engines — the same engines that powered the Space Shuttle — plus two massive solid rocket boosters generating 8.8 million pounds of thrust at liftoff.

Block 1 stands 322 feet tall, 15% more powerful than Saturn V. The later Block 1B adds an Exploration Upper Stage capable of lifting 105 metric tons to low Earth orbit.

Crew Capsule

Orion Capsule

Orion is built specifically for deep space — unlike Crew Dragon or Starliner which serve the ISS, Orion handles the radiation environment, long durations, and extreme re-entry speeds of lunar missions.

The European Space Agency provides the Service Module, supplying propulsion, power, and life support during transit. Returning from the Moon, Orion re-enters at 25,000 mph — faster than any crewed spacecraft since Apollo — with a heat shield reaching 5,000°F.

Lunar Outpost

Lunar Gateway

A small space station in a near-rectilinear halo orbit (NRHO) around the Moon — bringing it as close as 1,500 km above the surface. Unlike the ISS, Gateway is designed for intermittent crew visits rather than continuous habitation.

International partners contribute key modules: ESA and NASA provide the HALO habitation module, Canada provides a robotic arm, and JAXA handles logistics. The Power and Propulsion Element uses solar electric propulsion for efficient orbit maintenance.

Lunar Lander

Human Landing System (HLS)

NASA selected SpaceX's Starship as the Human Landing System — a modified version of the same rocket being developed for point-to-point Earth travel and eventual Mars missions. Starship HLS will be pre-positioned in lunar orbit before the crew arrives.

After docking with the Lunar Gateway, astronauts transfer to Starship HLS for the descent to the south pole. The vehicle uses liquid methane and liquid oxygen — propellants that could theoretically be produced from Martian resources.
